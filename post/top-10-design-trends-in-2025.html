Top 10 Not Bad design trends for 2025

Advice
February 8, 2025
As we enter a new year we like to reflect on the year past but also to geek out on the latest design trends. These trends can help you create designs that are fresh, modern, and engaging for your audience.

As we enter 2025, we find ourselves reflecting on the rapid evolution of design over the past few years. The design landscape continues to shift, driven by technological advancements, cultural changes, and a growing emphasis on sustainability and inclusivity. Staying ahead of these trends is key to creating designs that resonate with modern audiences. Here are the top 10 design trends we predict will dominate in 2025:

1) Immersive 3D and AR Experiences

3D design has evolved beyond static elements, now integrating seamlessly with augmented reality (AR). Brands are leveraging AR to create immersive experiences, allowing users to interact with products in real-time. For example, furniture retailers like IKEA are using AR to let customers visualise how pieces will look in their homes, while fashion brands are enabling virtual try-ons. Expect 3D and AR to become standard in web design, advertising, and e-commerce.

2) AI-Generated Design Elements

Artificial intelligence is revolutionising the creative process. AI tools are now capable of generating custom illustrations, layouts, and even entire branding systems. Designers are using AI to streamline workflows and create hyper-personalised content. For instance, platforms like Canva and Adobe Firefly are integrating AI to help users create professional designs in minutes. In 2025, AI will be a collaborative partner for designers, not just a tool.

3) Neo-Minimalism with a Twist

Minimalism is evolving into "neo-minimalism," where simplicity meets subtle complexity. Think clean layouts with unexpected pops of texture, micro-animations, or hidden interactive elements. Apple’s website remains a prime example, but now with more dynamic elements that engage users without overwhelming them. This trend emphasises clarity while keeping designs visually intriguing.

4) Dynamic Asymmetry and Fluid Layouts

Asymmetry is becoming more dynamic, with fluid, organic shapes and layouts that mimic natural movement. This trend breaks away from rigid grids, creating designs that feel alive and responsive. Brands like Spotify are using fluid layouts in their app interfaces, making the user experience feel more intuitive and engaging.

5) Hand-Crafted Digital Art

The demand for authenticity continues to grow, and hand-drawn elements are evolving into fully hand-crafted digital art. Designers are blending traditional techniques with digital tools to create unique, personalised visuals. For example, brands like Mailchimp are using hand-drawn illustrations to convey warmth and approachability, while luxury brands are incorporating hand-painted textures for a bespoke feel.

6) Retro-Futurism

The nostalgia for the past is merging with futuristic aesthetics, creating a trend called "retro-futurism." Think 80s and 90s-inspired designs reimagined with modern technology. This trend is evident in the resurgence of neon colours, pixel art, and glitch effects, as seen in the branding for tech start-ups and gaming platforms like Xbox and PlayStation.

7) Sustainable and Eco-Conscious Design

As environmental concerns grow, sustainable design is no longer optional—it’s essential. Brands are prioritising eco-friendly materials, minimalist packaging, and carbon-neutral production methods. Patagonia continues to lead the way, but now even tech companies like Google are incorporating sustainability into their branding, using earthy tones and nature-inspired visuals.

8) Hyper-Personalisation

With advancements in data analytics and AI, hyper-personalised design is becoming the norm. Websites and apps now adapt in real-time to user preferences, offering tailored experiences. For example, Netflix’s interface changes based on viewing habits, while e-commerce sites like Amazon display personalised product recommendations. In 2025, expect designs to feel uniquely "yours."

9) Dark Mode Dominance

Dark mode isn’t just a preference—it’s a design standard. With its sleek, modern look and reduced eye strain, dark mode is being adopted across apps, websites, and even operating systems. Brands like Twitter and Instagram have embraced it, and in 2025, it will be a default option for most digital platforms.

10) Ethical and Inclusive Design

Inclusivity and accessibility are at the forefront of design in 2025. Brands are prioritising designs that cater to diverse audiences, including those with disabilities. This includes colour-blind-friendly palettes, screen reader compatibility, and culturally sensitive visuals. Companies like Microsoft are leading the charge with their inclusive design principles, ensuring their products are accessible to everyone.

Conclusion  

The design world in 2025 is a blend of cutting-edge technology, timeless aesthetics, and a deep commitment to sustainability and inclusivity. By embracing these trends, designers can create work that is not only visually stunning but also meaningful and impactful. Whether it’s through immersive AR experiences, AI-generated art, or eco-conscious branding, the future of design is about connecting with audiences in innovative and authentic ways. Let’s make 2025 a year of creativity, purpose, and progress.
